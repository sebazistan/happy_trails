      How a day works

      Morning. The coop door opens at dawn. Open the pen gate so hens can range. Hens wake hungry. Take feed from the bin or the barn, then fill the feeder, or scatter feed on the ground and watch them peck it until it is gone. Fill the water trough from the pump (fill the can, then act at the trough). Hens with an empty belly or throat stop laying, and neglected hens eventually die.

      Daytime. Most hens lay in the coop nest boxes. Go inside the coop (the roof fades away) and collect from the nests. A few lay out in the yard. Collect eggs before they rot. Eggs do not sell themselves. Stock the farm stand and the roadside stall, and keep spares in the barn fridge. Customers buy cartons of six. Your wife serves at the farm stand from Monday to Friday and goes back to the house at night. The roadside stall runs on an honesty box. Money only counts once you collect it from the till or the box. The feed shop sells sacks that you drive back to the bin. Clean the coop when it gets dirty. Rain makes mud and sends hens under cover.

      Catching hens. Hens see mostly straight ahead, and they only hear you when you move without sneaking. Press C (or the Sneak button) to switch sneaking on, creep up behind a hen and pick her up. You can carry three, a little slower for each. Carry them to the pen and put them down. Yellow cones show what nearby hens can see while you sneak.

      Growing the farm. The pen holds about one hen per 4,400 square pixels and the coop has a fixed number of roosts. Hens that cannot roost sleep out in the pen, where foxes find them easily. A feeder lets three hens eat at once and a water trough lets two drink at once, and they empty faster the more hens you keep. Drive west off the end of the road to Emil & Gus Farm Supply in town. Buy fence sections, gates, feeders, troughs, coop lumber and feed, then drive home and unload at the bin or the barn. Press B for build mode: pick a tool, then click or tap the map to place. Remove puts a piece back in your supplies. Extend the coop from the plank sign on its east side.

      Dusk. Hens head for the coop. The door shuts later in the evening, so get every hen in, close the gate, and check the fence. A hen left outside is fox food.

      Pets. Your wife puts food in the dog and cat bowls by the front door every morning and evening, and warns you when the food runs low. Buy a month of dog food and cat food at Emil & Gus. A hungry dog wanders off for a few days and, if you keep neglecting him, leaves for good. Hungry cats start killing hens. Rats come for unattended eggs, mostly at night. Chase one, sneak up while it is eating, and catch it with F. Hens peck at them without much luck, the dog is too slow, and the cats are the real rat catchers, and a hungry cat is glad of the meal. Bugs only top up a hen's hunger; they never replace feed. Cats chase hens for fun now and then and hop over fences. The dog sleeps most of the night, but he will chase off a fox that comes close. He cannot catch the hawk that sometimes swoops in by day.

      Night. Foxes come. They dig under fence sections, slip through an open gate and steal eggs and hens. Walk at one to scare it off, then repair any hole in the fence. When your energy runs low, sleep in the farmhouse. If you pass out, you lose the night.

      Tuning and test tools

      Roadmap coverage: farmer and context actions (2), wander, flee with hysteresis, separation, state machine (3 to 5), day and night with coop door (6), gate escape (7), eggs (8), economy with truck and roadside stall (9), feed approach (10), fox AI with fence digging (16), fence repair (17), rain and shelter (16), mud (15, simple version). Left out: roof occlusion, hatching, vehicle physics, a real moisture map.
