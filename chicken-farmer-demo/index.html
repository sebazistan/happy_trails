    How a day works

    Morning. The coop door opens at dawn. Open the pen gate so hens can range. Hens wake hungry. Take feed from the bin or the barn, then fill the feeder, or scatter feed on the ground and watch them peck it until it is gone. Fill the water trough from the pump (fill the can, then act at the trough). Hens with an empty belly or throat stop laying, and neglected hens eventually die.

    Daytime. Most hens lay in the coop nest boxes. Go inside the coop (the roof fades away) and collect from the nests. A few lay out in the yard. Collect eggs before they rot. Sell at the farm stand for a flat price, or load the truck and drive it to the roadside stall, where the price changes each day. The feed shop sells sacks that you drive back to the bin. Clean the coop when it gets dirty. Rain makes mud and sends hens under cover.

    Dusk. Hens head for the coop. The door shuts later in the evening, so get every hen in, close the gate, and check the fence. A hen left outside is fox food.

    Night. Foxes come. They dig under fence sections, slip through an open gate and steal eggs and hens. Walk at one to scare it off, then repair any hole in the fence. When your energy runs low, sleep in the farmhouse. If you pass out, you lose the night.
