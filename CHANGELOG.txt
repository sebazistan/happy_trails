This history used to live at the top of beltline.html, because for a long time
that was the only finished page and every change landed on it. It does not live
there any more: beltline.html is generated from trails_data.py like every other
trail page, and a generated file is the wrong place to keep a record — the next
build throws it away.

So it is here, in the release folder, beside VERSION.txt. The numbering below is
the Beltline page's own, which is what the entries were written against; the
folder's version (site-assets-v1.x) is on VERSION.txt.

VERSION HISTORY
  8.9  THE CARD IS MOSTLY WORDS NOW. On a 1400x900 screen a stop showed three
       lines over four pages two versions ago, eight over two pages last
       version, and TEN IN ONE PAGE here — the Allan Rd entry is read straight
       through with no arrows to press. The picture is bigger than it was, not
       smaller.
       The waypoint number and the picture's caption moved onto the top left of
       the photograph. They were a row between the picture and the title, which
       cost 21 pixels and put two small grey things between a photograph and
       the heading that names it. Over the picture they cost nothing, and the
       TITLE NOW BEGINS THE MOMENT THE PICTURE ENDS. Both carry a shadow and a
       soft darkening under them, because over a photograph nothing can be
       relied on for contrast.
       Cost and priority moved into the row of figures, after From start. They
       had a panel of their own beside the title, which made them the loudest
       thing on the card; they are the third and fourth facts about a stop and
       now read as that. No border — the row already has a rule above it, and a
       box inside a box is one box too many.
       "Scroll for more" is gone. It said, at the foot of every card, that the
       page was still a scroll: worth saying once, not thirteen times, and it
       cost a row of the thing the card is shortest of.
       The paragraph text is a point smaller (15px to 14px), which is another
       line and a half in the same card.
       ON A PHONE the cap on how many lines may show was still 4, set when the
       card's chrome took a hundred pixels more than it does — so the card was
       finishing 52 pixels short of the readouts with the text still cut into
       four pages, because the cap and not the room was the limit. It is 7 now,
       and a stop reads in three pages rather than five.
       The stepper arrows line up with the card's own left edge rather than the
       screen's, and the card holds a clear strip at its foot for them. The
       scroll hint used to be that strip.
  8.8  A trail page's layer switches are one per row at every size.
       They were in two columns, which is right for the main map's panel — a
       dozen switches that would otherwise run off the bottom of a phone — and
       wrong for a trail page, which has two or three. Two columns made them a
       pair of narrow boxes with their names broken across lines, side by side,
       for a panel that was no shorter for it.
       The notes under each name come back on a phone as a result. They were
       dropped there as the price of fitting the main map's dozen into two
       columns; a trail page has the room now, and it needs them — "Tap a
       waypoint to open it" is the only place that says what Waypoint auto-load
       does. A phone on its side still loses them: there is no height to spare.
  8.7  MORE ROOM FOR THE WORDS, and the pictures the same size.
       A card on a 1400x900 screen was showing THREE lines of text at a time —
       the minimum the engine will go to — and turning a stop's words into four
       pages. It shows eight now, in two pages, and the photograph is one pixel
       shorter than it was. The room came from three places:
         · the card column started below the corner title even on a wide screen
           where the two cannot touch — the title ends around x=330 and the card
           is capped at 540px against the right edge. Past 1000px it now starts
           near the top of the map, which is 80 pixels.
         · the scroll hint had a rule above it, twelve pixels of air either side
           and a line of its own, costing 41 pixels — nearly two lines of the
           text it sat under. It has no rule and much less air; the facts row
           above it already draws a line, and a second one four millimetres
           below the first was drawing a box around nothing.
         · a few pixels each off the paddings, the title's margin, the pager and
           the facts row.
       --card-text-lines went from 7 to 10, or the extra room would have been
       left empty on a tall screen.
       LINKS WORK INSIDE A STOP'S TEXT, and the Beltline has four of them as
       worked examples. Underlined rather than only coloured, because over a
       photograph the card's background is not reliably dark; a link leaving the
       site gets a small ↗ drawn in the text, so it prints and scales.
       WAYPOINT AUTO-LOAD is a new switch in the layers panel, on by default.
       Off, nothing opens by itself and a stop opens when you tap it — which is
       the better way to LOOK at the map, since nothing covers it unless you ask.
       It is one term in one expression in the drawing loop, because everything
       about a card was already written in terms of "how much should this be
       showing".
       THE STEPPER ARROWS ARE VISIBLE ON A PHONE. The card is full width there
       and is deliberately laid over the rail, which buried the one control that
       is needed exactly when a card is up — greyed and unreachable for the whole
       walk. The arrows now sit above the card in its bottom-left corner, and the
       scroll hint has moved to the right to give them the lane. Eighteen pixels,
       which is the whole price of having both.
  8.6  The Wishful thinking page stops moving when it should not.
       THE SCROLLBAR NO LONGER SHIFTS THE PAGE. Filtering twelve cards down to
       one made the page short enough not to need a scrollbar, the scrollbar
       went away, and on Windows and Linux the whole layout jumped fifteen
       pixels sideways — everything moving at once, none of it for a reason.
       The reading pages now reserve the gutter whether or not they need it
       (scrollbar-gutter: stable, with the older always-show-the-track trick
       for browsers that do not know the property). The map pages do not: they
       never scroll, and a permanent gutter beside a full-bleed map is a stripe
       of nothing down the edge of the artwork.
       CARDS ARRIVE AND LEAVE PROPERLY. A card that stops matching now shrinks
       and fades for 150ms and is taken out of the layout after that, rather
       than blinking out and letting the ones behind it jump into the hole.
       Arriving is slower than leaving on purpose: you are waiting for what is
       left, not watching what has gone.
       The filtering also stopped doing sixty times the work it needed to. The
       sliders call it on every frame while they are being dragged, and almost
       every one of those frames asks for the same set of cards; it now
       compares what was asked for against what is already shown and returns
       early, so an animation can finish instead of restarting each frame.
  8.5  THE BELTLINE IS NO LONGER A SPECIAL CASE. It was a hand-written page —
       the same page as the other eleven, but maintained separately, so every
       change to the shared template had to be made twice and its three wishful
       stops had to be listed a second time for the Wishful thinking page to
       find them. There was a check that refused to build if those two lists
       disagreed; the check worked, and it was a symptom. A list that has to be
       guarded against itself should not be two lists.
       Everything that makes the Beltline itself — its words, its photographs,
       its videos, its text, its heights — is in trails_data.py now, and
       make_pages.py writes its page exactly as it writes the others: where a
       trail supplies real content the generator uses it, and where it does not
       the generator invents a placeholder. The regenerated page was checked
       against the hand-written one waypoint by waypoint, including every
       caption and every measured position along the route, and is identical.
       Two things about it still differ and both are now stated in its entry
       rather than assumed in four scripts: artwork="beltline" (a railpath is
       not a river, so make_beltline.py draws it) and real=True.
       DO NOT EDIT beltline.html. Edit its entry in trails_data.py.
       On a waypoint card, cost and priority moved from a band of their own to
       the title's line, smaller, on a panel lighter than the card rather than
       darker — as a band they were the loudest thing on the card, and a dark
       panel inside a dark card reads as a hole in it.
  8.4  EVERY STOP NOW HAS AN ADDRESS OF ITS OWN.
       beltline.html#moore-crossing opens this page at that stop with its
       card up, and turns the Wishful thinking layer on if the stop needs
       it — a link to something that is not built is exactly the link most
       worth sending, and it used to land on a page where the thing it was
       about was switched off. Walking or stepping past a stop rewrites
       the address to name it, so the link to where you are is always in
       the bar. replaceState rather than pushState, so a walk down a trail
       leaves ONE entry in the back button rather than one per waypoint.
       A WISHFUL STOP CARRIES TWO NEW FACTS: what it would cost to build
       and how badly it is wanted, each low, medium or high, each shown as
       a picture and a word — a Canadian coin for the cost, a dial for the
       priority. The three coins differ in size and edge as well as metal,
       and the three dials differ in the ANGLE of the needle, so neither
       depends on colour alone.
       All the site's drawings are in icons/ now. There was a legend/
       folder, and adding a second kind of drawing would have meant
       guessing which of two folders it belonged in.
       images/ is "trail link images"/, and the new "wishful link images"/
       holds one picture per proposal.
  8.3  the corner title and the type-and-length line above it no longer
       carry a drop shadow. It was there to hold them up over pale
       artwork, and it did, but a soft edge on type that size reads as a
       smudge rather than as depth. They are flat colour now. If a trail's
       artwork ever swallows the title, the veil under it is the thing to
       darken.
  8.2  Six changes, most of them elsewhere but all of them here too, since
       the stylesheet and the engine are shared.
       On a WISHFUL trail the Wishful thinking switch is now locked on and
       greyed: it hides the stops that are proposed rather than built, and
       on a trail where every stop is proposed it could only blank the page.
       The type-and-length line on those pages is the trails' own purple
       (#e84fff) instead of a pale lavender that vanished against the map.
       THE TRAILS NOW ALL RUN DOWNHILL AND DOWN THE PAGE. The Lower Don was
       walked from the harbour up to the forks, so its first stop sat at the
       bottom of the drawing and the journey went up the screen while the
       reader scrolled down. Reversing it turned up three more doing the
       same thing — the East Don, Etobicoke Creek and Highland Creek — and
       a fourth, the Humber, whose stops were right but whose description
       said the opposite. All five now read the way the page does. Two of
       them are descents, which is what the negative elevation readout was
       built for and the first time anything has used it.
       The pictures on the trails page and behind these opening cards are
       no longer crops of the map. They are drawn as a view along the trail
       from eye height — the photograph that will replace them — because
       the map was already on screen twice and a third copy said nothing.
       A wishful trail's picture has no path in it, only a worn line
       through the grass, which is the honest thing for it to show.
       The opening card is wider and deeper so that picture can be seen.
       The waypoint pin on the map was still the old red accent, sitting
       on top of the green ring the artwork draws underneath it. It is the
       route's colour now, which makes it purple on a wishful trail
       without this being written down twice.
  8.1  Four small things, three of them on other pages but all of them in
       the one stylesheet this page shares.
       The Buy me a coffee link in the white bar is now the site's orange
       (#e8503a) rather than the brand's yellow, which was close enough to
       white to be hard to read up there. On the dark panel, where the
       yellow reads perfectly well, it stays yellow — two variables,
       --coffee-nav and --coffee-ink, so the two places can differ.
       The corner title, kicker and buttons on a trail page are now drawn
       in light ink over the top veil. The veil was never ON TOP of them —
       it sits at z-index 1 and the interface at 3 — but dark ink on a
       darkened band reads exactly like a covered title, which is what it
       looked like and why it was reported.
       (The other two, on trails.html: the Open the trail buttons work
       again, and the columns now follow the number of trails.)
  8.0  PART of a larger set of changes — see the note that came with this
       build for what is done and what is not.
       Done here: the trail line and the elevation line are now the same
       green the trails are drawn in on the main map (#4ecf4e), because
       they are the same fact drawn twice. A wishful trail is purple
       (#e84fff) throughout instead — one class, body.trail-wishful, set
       from SETTINGS.trailKind. The red accent now has one clearly marked
       home at the top of happy-trails.css.
       Two arrows above the readouts step from waypoint to waypoint,
       greying out at each end, skipping any stop the Wishful thinking
       switch is currently hiding. Wishful thinking now starts OFF.
       Type and length share one line above the title. A short, light
       veil at the top of the map matches the heavier one at the bottom.
       The corner buttons carry icons — a back arrow, a download arrow, an
       external-link mark — and Download now offers THIS trail's drawing,
       filled in from SETTINGS.mapFile. Wikipedia and Google maps appear
       only when their links are set, which for most trails is not yet.
       The × on a waypoint card is properly centred: the glyph carries
       empty space beneath it, so the grid was centring the box rather
       than the ink.
  7.3  the artwork is redrawn, and it now has a wishful layer of its own.
       The demo drawing — mountains, contours, Lake Verrier — has gone.
       This one is drawn as what the Beltline IS: a RAILPATH. That means
       it looks unlike the nine river and corridor trails on purpose. A
       former rail alignment runs almost straight, with long easy curves
       rather than meanders, because that is what trains need; it cuts
       across the street grid instead of following a valley; and it is
       interrupted by the roads it once bridged. So: straight west to east
       across midtown, past the severance at Allan Rd, through the
       cemetery, then down into the Don valley alongside Mud Creek to the
       Brick Works — which is also why the trail loses 75 m.
       Three purple proposed crossings, one at each wishful stop.
       NOTHING ELSE CHANGED. All thirteen waypoints keep the marker names
       they always had (#wp-landing … #wp-hut), so every word, photograph
       and video still points exactly where it did.
  7.2  the Wishful thinking switch now also shows and hides a LAYER IN THE
       ARTWORK — the routes that do not exist yet, drawn like the trail but
       shorter and in purple: the bridge that would close a gap, the link
       that would join two valleys. SETTINGS.wishfulLayer names the group
       to look for and defaults to "#wishful", so an Illustrator layer
       named "wishful" works with no setting at all.
       A proposal and the stop that explains it now appear and disappear
       together, which is the point of the layer: what the trail IS, and
       what it COULD be, one switch apart.
       THIS PAGE'S ARTWORK HAS NO SUCH GROUP YET — it is still the demo
       drawing. Nothing is looked for twice and nothing breaks; add a
       "wishful" layer to the real Beltline drawing and it starts working
       with no code change. All nine placeholder trails have one.
  7.1  every map drawing now lives in maps/ — the two that were loose in
       the root have joined the nine placeholder ones. Only two things
       ever pointed at them (the main map's "Download map" link and a note
       in this header), because the drawing a page actually shows is
       inlined into the page itself; the file in maps/ is the source you
       edit and re-paste.
       satellite.jpg is gone, along with everything that pointed at it. It
       was a render of the demo terrain rather than a photograph of
       anywhere, so it was never going to survive the real artwork. The
       LAYER still works — set satelliteImage in a trail's settings and
       that trail grows the switch again. With it gone the layers panel is
       down to one switch, which takes the full width of the panel.
  7.0  this page is now DATA, not machinery. Everything about how a trail
       page works moved into trail-engine.js, which every trail shares —
       so a visitor downloads it once for the whole site, and a fix made
       there is a fix on all ten trails at the same time. The engine also
       writes the interface, so ten pages cannot drift into ten slightly
       different navigation bars.
       Every SETTING and every shared WORD moved with it, into DEFAULTS.
       A trail page now lists only what is different about it: this one is
       six settings and seven lines of words. That is why the file went
       from 1251 lines to a few hundred plus its artwork. The annotations
       did not go anywhere — they are in trail-engine.js, where they are
       read once instead of ten times.
       The pictures and video now come from media/beltline/ rather than
       from the Webflow CDN, so the site no longer breaks the day it moves
       host. THEY ARE NOT DOWNLOADED YET — this workspace has no outbound
       network. Run media/beltline/download.ps1 once and the folder fills
       itself; until then every picture falls back to the placeholder,
       which is what the page does for any missing file.
       Elevation gain shows as a NEGATIVE number on a trail that ends
       lower than it starts, with the label changing to match — this trail
       drops 75 m end to end and the ascent figure had been a permanent
       zero. Videos are no longer fetched ahead on a metered connection.
       The page keeps its own <title> rather than having it overwritten.
  6.1  the first card now ENTERS rather than being uncovered. It gets the
       same entrance as every other card — the quick fade and the short
       rise into place — over the same stretch of scroll (cardFade), timed
       to land exactly as the darkening finishes leaving. The trick is
       that it multiplies into the CARD's own value rather than into the
       layer holding the cards: the rise is worked out from that value, so
       doing it there brings the rise along with the fade. Fading the
       column, as 6.0 did, flattened both into a slow dissolve.
       The wait between that card landing and the map beginning to move is
       halved — 0.076 of a walk to 0.038. openingHold and openingLeavesBy
       do this together: their product is the panel's exit, which is
       unchanged at 0.094, and what is left over is the pause.
       "Scroll for more" now shows at every screen size, not only on a
       phone. Doing that turned up a quiet old bug: --card-chrome already
       counted the line's height inside the short-window block, which is
       not width-scoped — so a desktop window under 720px tall had been
       giving up 40px of picture for a line it was not showing. The
       arithmetic and the page now agree.
  6.0  the first waypoint card now arrives WITH the opening panel's exit
       rather than after it — see cardsArriveWith, and the note in 5.9
       below for why the previous arrangement made it disappear entirely.
       Also a tidy-up: the settings block said colours lived in
       trail-map-v4.0.css, a file that has not existed for several
       versions; the description of how the scroll is divided still said
       the opening screen "fades" and still mentioned a progress bar that
       was taken out in 4.7; and the stylesheet carried two rules for
       things no page produces (.tm-control on a trail page — the zoom
       controls belong to the map page and are .ht-control — and
       .tm-button.tm-active, which nothing has set for a long time).
       Checked and found clean: no unused setting, no unused word, no
       unreachable function, no orphaned id.
  5.9  the opening panel TRAVELS UP and off the top as you scroll rather
       than dissolving where it stands. It is the first thing the page
       does, so it is also the page showing you what it wants from you;
       and it takes its own darkening with it, uncovering the map from the
       bottom up. The darkening now thins to nothing before the panel's
       own bottom edge, so no hard line sweeps up the map as it leaves.
       The first card fades up through the back half of that movement, so
       it has arrived by the time the darkening has — cardsArriveWith.
       It has to hang off the PANEL and not off the walk starting: the
       first waypoint sits at the trailhead, so its own stretch of scroll
       is over within a moment of the walk beginning. 5.9 held the
       interface back until after the walk began, which did not delay the
       first card so much as delete it — its window had closed before the
       layer holding it was ever switched on, and it was never seen at all.
       "Scroll for more" at the foot of a card, on phones only, where the
       card fills the screen and a reader can reasonably think the page
       has stopped being a scroll. It is paid for in --card-chrome, so the
       picture gives back exactly the height the line takes and the card
       still fits its column.
       A phone held on its side now gets a "turn your phone upright"
       screen instead of a broken layout: a short, wide window has no room
       for the map and a waypoint at once, and the card ends up taller
       than the space it has. Purely a media query — orientation, height,
       width and a coarse pointer — so a laptop with a short window is
       never caught by it.
       AND THE SCROLL ITSELF. On a phone the address bar slides away the
       moment you scroll, and the window grows by 60-100px. The page was
       "1250 screenfuls" tall, so it re-measured itself mid-gesture and
       grew by about 1100px in one frame — and since progress along the
       trail is your position as a SHARE of the page, the walk lurched
       BACKWARDS by 1.3% exactly as you started. It is now measured once,
       in pixels: same test, the page does not move at all and the residual
       shift is 0.1%. A real resize still re-measures; an address bar is
       told from a rotation by size. See lockTheScroll().
       The other half of that complaint was a band of bare ground at the
       foot of the screen. That was not the address bar: the route starts
       near the bottom edge of the artwork, so centring the walking point
       put the edge of the paper on screen — 361px of it at the start of
       the walk, and more as the address bar retracted. The view now stops
       at the edge of the paper and lets the walking point sit off centre
       instead, as any map does when you drag it into its corner. Measured
       across the whole walk, at every screen size: zero.
  5.8  the rail of buttons no longer sits across the waypoint card on a
       narrow screen. The card is full width there and fills nearly the
       whole column, so the two cannot share the space; the card now takes
       it and is simply laid over the rail, the way a sheet covers what is
       under it. Nothing fades and nothing blinks — for the two thirds of
       the walk with no card up the buttons are where they always were.
       The rail is also lowered far enough that no card can cut a button
       in half, and by how much is MEASURED at startup rather than typed
       in: the cards are anchored to the bottom of their column, so a
       shorter card starts further down, and it is the lowest of those top
       edges the rail has to clear. Change the words or the pictures and
       it re-measures itself. See placeRail() and
       SETTINGS.railBelowCardsOnPhone.
       The panel keeps the top layer, so one you have opened is never
       buried by a card arriving under it, and its own x still closes it.
  5.7  closer in and slower: zoomWalking is 1.25 and zoomWalkingPhone 0.82,
       and because zooming in alone would only make the map travel faster
       for the same flick of a thumb, the page grew with it — pageHeight
       1250 rather than 900, so there is half again as much scrolling
       between one waypoint and the next.
       A phone now shows each card for less of the walk than a laptop
       does: cardVisibleForPhone, with cardLeadPhone and cardFadePhone
       beside it. On a laptop the card sits in a column next to the map
       and you can read it and watch the route at once; on a phone it
       covers the screen, so the same figure meant spending the walk
       looking at a card. A card is now up for about a third of the walk
       on a phone instead of about two thirds. Nothing changed on desktop.
       flattenWidth is 3600 to match the artwork's own width, since the
       deeper zoom would otherwise have been blowing the flat picture up
       past its size.
  5.6  the file names have lost their numbers — this is beltline.html now,
       beside index.html, trails.html, about.html and contact.html, so a
       link never has to be rewritten when a version changes. The version
       lives in this header and in SETTINGS.version instead.
       The page now OPENS standing at the trailhead: the fly-in is gone
       (zoomOutAtStart), and with it the stretch of scroll it used. The
       opening words sit in the same box the finish used to use, with no
       blur behind them, and the walk cannot start until that box has
       completely gone. At the other end the pull-back is longer, eased
       more gently and blended by ratio rather than by difference, which
       is what makes it read as one steady movement; the closing panel and
       the blur behind it are gone, so the page finishes on the whole map
       with nothing over it.
       And it is markedly faster on a phone. Three things were costing
       most of the frames: the frosted glass behind the card and the
       panels, which is a full blur of the moving map on every frame and
       is now off on phones; the full-screen blur at each end, now off
       everywhere by default; and the artwork itself, every contour line
       of which was re-traced from its outline on every frame. That last
       one is now flattened into a single picture in the background, a
       moment after the page loads, and the picture is what moves. On a
       throttled mid-range phone the walk went from about 10 frames a
       second to about 40. The per-frame arithmetic was tightened as
       well: line weights and chip sizes are only rewritten when the zoom
       actually changes, the climb total is added up once instead of
       every frame, the elevation line is extended rather than rebuilt,
       and a readout is only written when its number has changed.
  5.5  a turning ring over a video while it is still filling its buffer,
       and videos near the card you are looking at are fetched ahead so
       they do not stutter on first play. A layer group holding a single
       switch takes the full width of the panel.
